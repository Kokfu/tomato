# V8.4 Real-world GPS navigation

Scope: GPS driving navigation between real Kuching landmarks on the mapped OSM road network. No new vehicles, traffic,
rendering engine, buildings, landmarks, geographic coordinates or paid / online services. No automated driving and no
teleporting in play. Shader preparation (V8.1) and chunk scheduling (V8.2) are untouched.

Status: implemented, tested, committed and pushed to `v5-rendering-experiment`; not deployed by hand.

## 1. Road routing investigation (Task 1)

What existed: a pedestrian routing graph (`graph.json`) and router (`src/ui/Router.ts`, A*, snapping, turn hints) for
walking routes, and per-chunk road pieces with a one-way flag that the car's road query uses. The walking graph is
unusable for cars: it holds footways, pedestrian streets, steps and the Darul Hana footbridge, and ignores one-way.

| Question | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Real connectivity and intersections | Yes: topology comes from shared OSM node ids only; nothing is snapped or invented. Ways that only touch visually stay disconnected. |
| One-way restrictions | Yes: 2 327 ways `oneway=yes`, 42 `junction=roundabout`, 10 `circular`; no `oneway=-1` in the extract (supported anyway). Now honoured (4 073 one-way edges). |
| Vehicle access | Partly mapped: `access=private` 81, `no` 10, `motor_vehicle=no|private` 7, `service=emergency_access` 3. These 99 ways are excluded. `access=destination|customers|permissive` are allowed. |
| Pedestrian-only streets | Mapped as `highway=pedestrian` / `footway` etc.; never in the car graph. Jalan India is a pedestrian *area* with no centre line in the extract. |
| Bridges | 27 bridge edges, all on the main network (Satok, Tun Abdul Rahman Ya'kub, Datuk Amar Juma'ani and smaller ones). Jambatan Darul Hana is pedestrian: never driven. |
| Turn restrictions | **Not available.** The extract holds only multipolygon and waterway relations (no `type=restriction`), so no-left-turn / no-U-turn rules are not known and not guessed. `turn:lanes` is on 1 way. |
| Road surface vs centre line | Routing is on OSM centre lines. Widths are inferred for almost every way, so a lane can be metres off the line; snapping and progress use the nearest point of the line with generous thresholds (40 m start, 22 m off-route). |
| V7 Jalan China | Its OSM line runs ~2.6 m east of the street middle (through the east parked row). Navigation works with the car in the real lane; the e2e drive does exactly that. |
| Roads through buildings | 85 drivable segments that pass through solid footprints are dropped, as in the walking graph. A covered `building_passage` driveway into the DUN building survived (footprint data) and is excluded as a stopping point. |

## 2. What was built

- **Car routing graph** `public/data/kuching/drive-graph.json` (474 kB, loaded the first time the GPS opens):
  `node tools/generate-world/build-graph.ts --drive`, now part of `npm run data:graph`. Same builder, water and
  building rules as the walking graph; drivable classes only, width rule of the car's road query, access exclusions,
  directed one-way edges. 9 813 nodes, 12 043 edges, 633 km, 43 components (largest 97.5 %). `graph.json` is
  byte-identical to before.
- **Router** (`src/ui/Router.ts`): one-way-aware A* (seeds, goal and traversal), drive cost profile (major roads
  preferred), a U-turn penalty against the car's direction of travel, candidate snap pairs retried when a directed
  search fails, a stopping-point filter (`isStoppingEdge`: no `service`, no covered ways) and `trimEndConnector`.
  Walking behaviour is unchanged (all existing router tests pass).
- **Navigation** (`src/ui/Minimap.ts`): routes carry a mode. Drive mode plans from the car (within 40 m of a mapped
  carriageway) to the stopping point, measures the walk left on the walking graph, shows next turn / remaining road
  distance, re-plans after 3 checks more than 22 m off the route, and reports arrival with the walk left. Getting out
  turns the route into a walking route; getting back in turns an unfinished walk into a drive.
- **GPS panel** (`src/ui/GpsPanel.ts`): `G` or the GPS button at the wheel. Five destinations with road distance and
  walk, or the reason they are unavailable (disabled). Cancel in the panel, the hint's `✕` or `X`.
- **HUD / maps**: magenta route line (walking stays blue) on minimap and full map, magenta-and-white stopping-point
  disc, turn glyph in the hint, amber pointer when there is no route. Speedometer and context controls unchanged
  (controls line gains `G GPS`). On touch the button sits above the speedometer, clear of joystick and buttons.

Destinations from the car start on Jalan China:

| Destination | Road | Stops on | Walk after parking |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lau Ya Keng Food Court | 680 m | Jalan Carpenter | 11 m |

The north-bank sites have no road bridge near the waterfront (Darul Hana is pedestrian), so the GPS honestly sends the
car round by the Satok bridge and says how far the walk is.

## 4. Performance vs V8.3

`node tools/dev/drive-bench.ts --scenarios=C,D --runs=2 [--gps=fort-margherita]` (new `--gps` option), production builds,
Intel UHD (ANGLE D3D11), medium preset, V8.3 (`4d4bd1b`) and V8.4 alternated, two pairs. In the V8.4 runs a GPS route
to Fort Margherita stays active; route D drives away from it, so it re-plans 5-7 times per run (worst case). Raw data:
`docs/perf-v8.4.json`.

| | V8.3 C | V8.4+GPS C | V8.3 D | V8.4+GPS D |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| avg fps | 54.1 | 56.1 | 59.3 | 58.8 |
| median frame | 17.1 ms | 16.8 ms | 16.7 ms | 16.7 ms |
| p95 / p99 | 25.4 / 31.2 | 23.4 / 29.5 | 17.7 / 26.3 | 18.1 / 31.9 |
| frames > 50 ms per run | 2-7 | 5-15 | 3-8 | 8-19 |
| minimap + GPS per frame | 0.11 ms | 0.17 ms | 0.06 ms | 0.13 ms |
| GPS/minimap share of all slow-frame time | <= 1 ms | <= 6.2 ms | <= 1 ms | <= 2.2 ms |
| peak JS heap | 98-139 MB | 117-156 MB | 84-130 MB | 97-131 MB |

- First route (drive graph decode included): 22-25 ms, once, when the GPS opens. A re-plan of the 11 km route:
  10-16 ms, 7.5-11 ms after caching the walk from the stopping point (it rarely changes).
- Medians and averages are unchanged. Frames over 50 ms are more frequent in the GPS runs, but the GPS code is not in
  them (at most 6 ms of their total time); the rest is rendering time not attributed to any system.
- **Isolation run**: the same V8.4 build with GPS off, measured last: C 12 / 47 and D 16 / 17 frames over 50 ms
  (cold / warm), 89-122 MB heap, minimap 0.06 ms/frame on D. So the slow-frame difference is machine variance late in
  the session (as in V8.2, where D varied 11-27 on one build), not the GPS. The GPS costs ~0.07 ms per frame plus a
  7-16 ms re-plan when the driver leaves the route, and ~474 kB (drive graph) + ~505 kB (walking graph) loaded on first
  use.

## 5. Known map-data limitations

- **No turn restrictions** in the data: routes may include turns that are banned on the ground.
- **Access is only as good as the tags**: unmapped private roads and gated compounds are routable; `service` roads are
  never stopping points, which pushes the Fort Margherita and DUN stops to public roads ~530 m walk away (their own car
  parks are on service roads that cannot be confirmed as public).
- **Centre lines, not lanes**: no lane geometry, lane counts or junction shapes; turns are announced at the OSM
  junction node. Inferred widths mean the car's lane can be several metres off the line.
- **Old-town one-way loops are real**: Main Bazaar from Jalan China is 2.2 km because Main Bazaar is one-way; the
  route is what the data says.
- **Building check uses generic footprints**: 85 drivable segments through footprints are dropped, which can cut a
  covered car park or passage; one covered passage (DUN) remains in the graph but is never a stopping point.
- **Destinations are landmark points** (often building centroids); "walk" is measured on mapped footways and roads.
- Isolated pieces (43 components; 2.5 % of the length) cannot be reached from the main network; the GPS reports
  "No mapped road connection" for them.
- Streets marked one-way are driveable in the wrong direction in the physics (no enforcement), only the GPS respects
  them.
